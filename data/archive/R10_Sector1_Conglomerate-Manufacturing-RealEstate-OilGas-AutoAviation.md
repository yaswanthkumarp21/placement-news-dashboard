## 1. Sector operations overview

1. **Process industries** (refining, steel, aluminium, cement, chemicals) run continuous, asset-heavy plants where utilisation, energy cost and raw-material security decide margins. Examples: Reliance at Jamnagar (two refineries, 660,000 + 704,000 bpd), UltraTech (204.7 mtpa), Hindalco, JSPL, Evonith (1.4 mtpa). ([Reuters via Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/indias-reliance-buy-sanctions-compliant-132556976.html); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/capital-market-news/ultratech-cement-commissions-4-6-mtpa-cement-capacity-126092400165_1.html))
2. **Discrete manufacturing** (cars, components, electrical equipment, tyres) is about line balancing, supplier parks, JIT and quality. Maruti added 500,000 units of capacity in 2026 to reach about 2.9 m. ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/industry/story/maruti-suzuki-starts-production-at-fourth-gujarat-plant-takes-total-annual-capacity-to-2-9-million-units-546238-2026-07-30))
3. **Energy transition manufacturing** (solar cells and modules, batteries, grid equipment) is the fastest-growing capex area, pushed by ALMM rules and PLI incentives. Examples: Reliance, Tata Power, Vikram Solar, GE Vernova, Schneider.
4. **Power:** demand hit a record 271 GW in May 2026, with the government planning for about 300 GW next year. ([Energy Watch](https://www.energywatch.in/power/india-met-record-271-gw-peak-power-demand-in-may-gearing-up-for-300-gw-next-year-khattar))
5. **Aviation operations** are constrained by crew rules (FDTL), engine availability and airspace. IndiGo's December 2025 crisis and the 2026 West Asia closures show both.
6. **Real estate and hospitality** operations are project execution (labour, approvals, contractor management) and asset management (occupancy, energy). Labour shortages and minimum-wage increases hit construction in 2026.
7. **Freight and logistics:** rail is gaining share for bulk and finished vehicles (Maruti at 26.5% by rail). Ocean freight and war-risk insurance spiked in 2026.
8. **What changed in 2026:**
   - Input-cost volatility from the war.
   - New trade terms with the US and EU.
   - Carbon reporting under CBAM.
   - Captive power and coal security moving up the agenda.
   - Labour-code compliance costs.

## 2. Industry-wide developments (oldest first)

### [2026-01-01] EU CBAM definitive phase begins: carbon charges on steel, aluminium and cement imports
- **Recap:** From 1 January 2026, the EU's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism moved to its definitive phase, charging for embedded carbon in imports from energy-intensive sectors. Exporters need verified plant-level emissions data. GTRI estimates Indian exporters may need to cut prices 15–22% to absorb the cost. The India–EU FTA (27 January) gave no CBAM exemption.
- **Source:** [Vajiram & Ravi](https://vajiramandravi.com/current-affairs/eus-cbam-begins-impact-on-indias-steel-and-aluminium-exports/); additional: [European Commission](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism_en); [S&P Global (no exemption)](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/metals/012726-india-eu-agreement-cuts-car-tariffs-as-eu-eyes-4-million-vehicle-market)
- **Source quality:** Official (EU) / Trade press
- **Confidence:** Corroborated (the 15–22% figure is one think-tank estimate)
- **Theme:** Cost, sourcing & trade policy
- **Role relevance:** Plant operations / Supply chain
- **Why it matters:** Plant emissions become a trade cost. Operations teams must measure emissions per tonne by plant and decide on scrap-based EAF, green power and fuel switching.
- **Key numbers:** 15–22% possible price cut (GTRI).
- **Companies in this group most affected:** JSPL, Aditya Birla Group (Hindalco), Evonth Steel (Evonith), Saint Gobain.

- **Confidence:** Corroborated
- **Theme:** Cost, sourcing & trade policy
- **Role relevance:** Plant operations / Other (HR operations, EHS)
- **Why it matters:** Higher gratuity and wage-base costs change the economics of contract vs permanent labour on shop floors and construction sites. OSH rules add safety compliance.
- **Key numbers:** Effective 21 Nov 2025; Maruti ₹594 cr one-time charge.
- **Companies in this group most affected:** Maruti Suzuki, Bosch, Godrej & Boyce, IndiGo, Sobha, all manufacturers with large workforces.

## 3. Top 10 GD topics for Sector 1

1. **"Should India build strategic reserves for LPG and gas, not just crude?"**
   - **For:** 91% of February LPG imports came from the Gulf, and only about 41% of demand is met locally. Rationing (25-day booking) shows the risk.
   - **Against:** reserves are costly. Diversified suppliers, plus refiners' ability to raise LPG output (+25–28%), may be enough.
   - **Facts:** S&P Global; News On AIR (March 2026).
2. **"Has the West Asia war shown that Indian manufacturing is too exposed to imported inputs?"**
   - **For:** Maruti's margin fell from 8.8% to 5.1%; UltraTech faced its "sharpest imported fuel cost shock"; freight rose about tenfold.
   - **Against:** firms coped by passing costs on, switching fuels and re-routing exports (Maruti exports +28.6%). Volumes held.
   - **Facts:** Q1 FY27 calls.
3. **"CBAM: a climate tool or a trade barrier for India?"**
   - **For (barrier):** exporters may need 15–22% price cuts, and the EU FTA gave no exemption.
   - **Against:** it pushes Indian steel and aluminium toward EAF and green power, which buyers will demand anyway.
   - **Facts:** GTRI estimate; India–EU FTA (27 January 2026).
4. **"India–EU FTA: will 10% car tariffs hurt or help Indian auto?"**
