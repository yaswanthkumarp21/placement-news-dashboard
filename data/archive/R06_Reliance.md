### [2026-01-02] Jamnagar resumes Russian crude via non-sanctioned sellers; runs it through the domestic refinery to protect EU exports
- **Recap:** After pausing following US sanctions on Rosneft and Lukoil, about 2.2 m barrels of Urals were reported heading to Jamnagar in January from non-sanctioned spot sellers. Reuters reported further sanctions-compliant cargoes for February and March. Because the EU from 21 January 2026 bars fuel from refineries that processed Russian crude in the prior 60 days, Reliance planned to process Russian barrels in its 660,000 bpd domestic refinery and keep the 704,000 bpd export refinery Russian-free.

- **Source quality:** Business press (Reuters)
- **Confidence:** Corroborated
- **Theme:** Cost, sourcing & trade policy
- **Role relevance:** Procurement / Supply chain
- **Why it matters:** **Best procurement case in this file.** Splitting crude across two refineries to meet two sanctions regimes is crude-slate planning under regulation. Expect "how would you keep cheap crude and still export to Europe."
- **Key numbers:** ~2.2 m bbl Urals; 660,000 bpd domestic and 704,000 bpd export refineries; EU 60-day rule from 21 Jan 2026.

### [2026-03-10] Hormuz crisis: Reliance maximises LPG at Jamnagar and diverts KG-D6 gas to priority sectors
- **Recap:** After a government directive, Reliance said it would maximise LPG output from its Jamnagar refining and petrochemical complexes and send KG-D6 gas to priority sectors. - **Confidence:** Corroborated

- **Role relevance:** Plant operations / Supply chain
- **Why it matters:** Changing a refinery's product slate (propane and butane to LPG instead of petrochemical feed) trades margin for national supply. The Q1 FY27 call said LPG output rose about fourfold from February. Expect "how would you re-plan a refinery's product mix in a crisis."
- **Key numbers:** 41% domestic LPG self-sufficiency; 91% of imports from the Gulf; 25–28% national LPG output rise.
