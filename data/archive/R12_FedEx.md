## Operations deep dive

**Manufacturing footprint and capacity (network).** Network 2.0 merging Express and Ground stations (~490 optimised, 45% of volume); air fleet down a net 34 jets since FY22; MD-11s returning; India: Bengaluru (2025), Navi Mumbai (₹2,500 cr) and Delhi ($150 m) automated hubs. ([FedEx Q4 FY26 transcript](https://s21.q4cdn.com/665674268/files/doc_financials/2026/q4/Earnings-Transcript-Q4FY26.pdf))

**Supply chain and procurement.** Purchased transportation and wages are the main cost inflation ($2.6 bn headwind). Fuel is passed through with a weekly surcharge. Air capacity moved from trans-Pacific to Asia–Europe and intra-Asia.

**Cost structure and input costs.** Transformation savings above $1 bn; Network 2.0 adds $1 bn by end-2026 and $2 bn by end-2027. Capex is held at about 4% of revenue to 2029.

**Technology, automation and quality.** AI embedded in the DRIVE process; about two petabytes of data a day; automated sorting in Indian hubs (5,000 packages an hour in Delhi); InPost lockers for automated last mile.

## Likely operations interview questions

1. **"Why merge two delivery networks (Express and Ground)?"** Fewer stations, one truck per route, higher density; risks are service disruption and labour integration.
2. **"How would you re-allocate air capacity when one lane collapses?"** Lane profitability, demand forecast, aircraft range, crew and slot availability.
3. **"Design an air cargo hub for India's exports."** Sort capacity, customs, cold chain, security screening, peak buffers.
4. **"Lockers vs doorstep: when do lockers win?"** Density, failed-delivery rates, returns, customer convenience.
5. **"How would you protect service when a transit hub closes?"** Alternate routings, customer communication, prioritise high-value shipments, surcharges.
6. **"How do you keep capex low while growing?"** Utilise existing assets, automation with fast payback, lease vs buy, retire old fleet.
