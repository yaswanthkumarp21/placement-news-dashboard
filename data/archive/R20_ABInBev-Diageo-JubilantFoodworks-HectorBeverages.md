## Operations deep dive

**Manufacturing footprint and capacity.** AB InBev operates across a distributed multi-plant model in India, necessitated by state-level excise regulations that levy prohibitive import duties on inter-state beer movement. Its latest asset addition—Rochees Breweries Limited in Neemrana, Rajasthan (operational July 2026, ₹200 crore investment)—expands Northern India capacity. The company invests an average of ~$25 million annually into high-speed canning lines and cellar modernisation. Global brands such as Budweiser, Hoegaarden, and Corona are brewed locally across certified Indian plants to eliminate 150% import tariffs.

**Supply chain and procurement.** The core raw material supply chain focuses on barley, water, glass, and aluminium. AB InBev’s SmartBarley initiative localized over 16,000 tonnes of domestic two-row malting barley in 2026 (+30% YoY) across 2,000 contracted farmers in Rajasthan, Haryana, and UP, insulating the business from international agricultural inflation. On packaging, the company has strategically shifted its portfolio mix toward aluminium cans (>40% of sales vs 28% industry average), simplifying outbound freight, increasing transport cube utilisation, and eliminating the expensive reverse supply chain of returning empty glass bottles.

**Cost structure and input costs.** In beer manufacturing, packaging (glass bottles, aluminium cans, crowns, corrugated cartons) represents 40–45% of cost of goods sold, often exceeding raw material agricultural inputs (malt, hops, yeast). Glass price volatility and power/fuel costs (furnace oil/natural gas in glass factories) have pressured margins, prompting the strategic transition to aluminium cans. State-level excise duties, license fees, and vend fees remain the single largest cost wedge between ex-factory cost and consumer retail price.

**Technology, automation and quality.** AB InBev’s digital B2B engine, BEES, coordinates daily distributor replenishment and retailer order capture, replacing manual sales rep order-booking with algorithmic inventory suggestions. In plants, automation focuses on automated clean-in-place (CIP) brewing cycles, dissolved oxygen (DO) control sensors to protect beer freshness, and automated high-speed canning/seaming inspection cameras.

## Likely operations interview questions

1. **"How does the state excise regime in India dictate brewery network design and plant location decisions?"**  
   *Answer outline:* Inter-state alcohol transit attracts import duties, transit permits, and border checkpoints, making a centralised mega-plant unviable. Brewers must adopt a distributed footprint of owned and contract-manufacturing breweries across major consumption states to brew within state borders.
2. **"Why is AB InBev aggressively shifting its packaging mix toward aluminium cans over glass bottles?"**  
   *Answer outline:* Cans provide higher volumetric efficiency in trucking, lower tare weight, zero in-transit shatter risk, and eliminate the working capital and logistics cost of returnable glass bottle collection and washing.
3. **"How does contract farming under the SmartBarley program insulate the brewing supply chain?"**  
   *Answer outline:* Contract farming locks in predictable volumes and malt-grade specifications at pre-agreed farmgate pricing, de-risking the company from global barley price spikes, FX fluctuations, and import logistics lead times.
4. **"What operational challenges arise when localising global beer brands like Corona or Hoegaarden in India?"**  
   *Answer outline:* Maintaining brand sensory parity requires strict quality control over water profile (reverse osmosis demineralization), yeast strain propagation, cold-chain transport of live yeast, and specialized brewing certifications.
5. **"How does digital B2B ordering (BEES) impact supply chain forecasting and working capital?"**  
   *Answer outline:* Replacing manual sales order cycles with real-time digital ordering eliminates bullwhip distortions, provides granular store-level demand signals, and enables dynamic stock replenishment at distributor depots.
6. **"How would an operations team manage sudden 40% volume surges like the one in Maharashtra following excise policy changes?"**  
   *Answer outline:* Bottlenecks typically occur in fermentation and maturation cellar tank turnaround; operations must optimize high-gravity brewing, schedule round-the-clock packaging shifts, and secure packaging buffer stocks (cans/cartons).

## Operations deep dive

**Manufacturing footprint and capacity.** Hector Beverages operates two owned mother plants: Manesar, Haryana (~80 pouches/min) and Mysuru, Karnataka (~380–410 units/min), totaling approximately 460 units/minute of owned capacity. However, as demonstrated by FY26 filings, the company has pivoted heavily toward contract manufacturing: traded/co-packed goods now represent over 75% of total operating revenue (₹574 crore), while own factory production dropped to ₹184 crore. This network of third-party bottling and pouch-filling partners allows regional scaling without heavy plant capex.

**Supply chain and procurement.** Agricultural raw material sourcing is highly seasonal. Paper Boat’s signature beverages depend on mango pulp (Totapuri and Alphonso), lemon juice, ginger, tamarind, and kokum. Sourcing involves seasonal procurement agreements during peak summer harvest windows, converting fruit into aseptic pulp drums for year-round production. Packaging procurement is complex: the brand’s iconic Doypack stand-up pouch uses a multi-layered laminate (PET, aluminium foil, nylon, polyethylene) that allows retort sterilisation without chemical preservatives.

**Cost structure and input costs.** In FY26, raw materials and packaging represented ₹485 crore, or 62.5% of total company expenditure (₹776 crore). Selling and distribution expenses accounted for ₹67 crore (+15.5%), while marketing was ₹28 crore (+55.6%). The beverage business has low gross margin tolerance for fruit pulp or packaging laminate price hikes; when material costs rose in FY26, EBITDA margins contracted to 5.4%, and net profit dropped by 96%.

**Technology, automation and quality.** Quality assurance in retort pouches requires strict thermal process validation: after filling, pouches undergo pressurized steam retorting to achieve commercial sterility, extending ambient shelf life without chemical additives. Quality control tracks seal integrity, burst pressure testing, and barrier oxygen transmission rates. In sales operations, distributor automation systems track primary billing from C&Fs to distributors and monitor retail trade inventory turn-rates.

## Likely operations interview questions

1. **"Why did Hector Beverages shift over 75% of its production to contract manufacturing and traded goods in FY26?"**  
   *Answer outline:* To scale national distribution without investing heavy capital in regional bottling plants, reducing fixed depreciation and transport costs for heavy liquid freight, though at the expense of co-packer margin sharing.
2. **"How does the procurement team manage the seasonal availability of mangoes and agricultural inputs for Paper Boat?"**  
   *Answer outline:* By contracting with fruit processing hubs (e.g. Krishnagiri/Chittoor) during the May–July harvest, processing fresh fruit into aseptically packaged pulp drums, and storing them in ambient/cold warehouses for staggered year-round production.
3. **"What caused Paper Boat’s profit to plunge 96% in FY26 despite 13.8% topline revenue growth?"**  
   *Answer outline:* Inability to pass on material cost increases (₹485 crore, 62.5% of costs), coupled with rising freight/distribution expenses (+15.5%) and aggressive marketing spend (+55.6%), severely compressing operating leverage.
4. **"What quality control challenges arise when producing retort pouches across external co-packers?"**  
   *Answer outline:* Maintaining retort cook-time and temperature validation (F0 values), preventing pouch micro-leaks during hot filling and sealing, and ensuring identical sensory/viscosity profiles across geographically dispersed facilities.
5. **"How does quick commerce (Blinkit/Zepto) change distribution planning for a beverage company compared to general trade?"**  
   *Answer outline:* General trade works on weekly distributor replenishment cycles; quick commerce requires near real-time EDI order integration, high fill rates, smaller drop sizes, and localized buffer inventory near urban dark store clusters.
6. **"What are the operational advantages and disadvantages of retort pouches over PET bottles for fruit beverages?"**  
   *Answer outline:* Pouches provide superior light/oxygen barriers, longer shelf life without preservatives, and a distinctive brand silhouette; however, filling speeds (80–400 ppm) are much slower than high-speed PET lines (600–1,000+ ppm) and pouches cannot be re-sealed once opened.
